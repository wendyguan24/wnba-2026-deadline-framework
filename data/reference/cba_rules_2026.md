# WNBA CBA (2026-2033): framework reference notes

Internal reference for the WNBA 2026 trade-deadline framework, scoped to the
rules the framework actually uses: cap context, trade feasibility, contract
typology, and the mid-season break. Rules are paraphrased in plain language
with citations; this is not a substitute for the CBA text, and it does not
reproduce it. Any figure or rule that reaches `output/` must be re-verified
against the cited Article/Section at publish time (AMENDMENT_02 pre-publish
checklist items 2 and 3).

Sources: (1) WNBA Collective Bargaining Agreement (2026-2033), full text, for all
CBA rules and dollar figures; (2) Her Hoop Stats WNBA CBA FAQ (herhoopstats.com),
for plain-language explanation of supermax eligibility. Where the two conflict on
figures, the CBA governs (see Sec. 6).
As of: 2026-07-20. CBA line numbers refer to the local txt conversion used to build
this note, for quick re-lookup; cite the Article/Section (not the line) in any
published output.

## 1. Salary cap and team salary (Article VII)

- 2026 Salary Cap: $7,000,000 per team (Art. VII Sec. 1(a), line 3277). This
  matches the cap-tracker maximum.
- Escalation: the 2027 cap may move at most 13% from 2026, and each year after
  that at most 10% year over year (Art. VII Sec. 1(a), lines 3286-3290). So the
  near-term cap is stable and predictable, not a moving target.
- Minimum Team Salary (floor): 85% of the cap, which is $5,950,000 for 2026
  (Art. VII Sec. 1(c), lines 3331-3332). A team below the floor pays the
  shortfall out to its players.
- "Salary Cap" is defined as the maximum allowable Team Salary (Art. I
  definitions, line 996).

## 2. Hard cap and the trade-fitting rule (Article VII) [checklist item 2]

- The WNBA is a hard-cap league: a team's Team Salary may not exceed the Salary
  Cap at any time (Art. VII, line 3584).
- To take on salary, by signing or by trade, a team must have Room (cap space
  below the cap) or a qualifying Exception (Art. I definition of Room, line 968;
  Art. VII, lines 3592-3595). There is no going over the cap to absorb a
  contract.
- Framework implication: a "capped" team (Team Salary at or over the cap, no
  Room) cannot acquire salary without shedding equal or greater salary first.
  This is the CBA basis for R/08's feasibility-conditioned lever: an "acquire"
  read for a capped team must become "acquire (constrained: requires salary
  out)" or downgrade, never a move the cap forbids. Ties to AMENDMENT_02 Sec. 3b.

## 3. Core Player designation (Article VI): the WNBA "franchise tag" analogue

- A team may designate a Veteran Free Agent as a Core Player (Art. VI Sec. 7,
  lines 3034 onward). While she is designated, only that team may negotiate with
  or sign her (line 3075).
- Limits: the number of designations is capped; a player may not be Cored after
  the season in which she accrues her seventh year of service; a Core contract
  runs two or more seasons; the team must have Room to designate (lines
  3052-3056, 3097).
- Tradeability nuance (important): a Core Player can still be traded. The Core
  Designation terminates when her contract is assigned to another team (line
  3085). So the Core tag restricts a player's free-agent movement (only her own
  team may sign her), not her tradeability.

## 4. Contract protection and guarantees (Article V) [checklist item 3]

- WNBA contracts are not automatically fully guaranteed. Guarantee comes through
  negotiated Base Salary Protection (Art. V, lines 1493 onward): a team may
  protect Base Salary against termination for lack of skill and/or injury or
  illness, per Exhibit 2 of the Standard Player Contract.
- Timing: Base Salary becomes fully protected if the team does not request
  waivers before the first day of the relevant second regular season window
  (lines 1472-1486). Qualifying Offers must be fully protected for lack of skill
  and for injury or illness (line 863).
- Framework implication: whether a named candidate's salary is guaranteed or
  protected is contract-specific and must be verified per player before her
  contract security is characterized (AMENDMENT_02 Sec. 3c, checklist item 3).
  Do not assume full guarantees.

## 5. International Tournament Hiatus and Prioritization (Articles XL, VII Sec. 9): the "World Cup break"

- 2026 mid-season Hiatus: the 2026 season includes a Hiatus for the 2026 FIBA
  World Cup. Players may leave after their August 26, 2026 game (or the team's
  last game before August 26) to join national-team training, and must return
  before the Hiatus ends (Art. VII Sec. 9(c), lines 6269-6273).
- Prioritization: veterans (three or more years of service) must prioritize the
  WNBA, meaning report by season start and do not depart mid-season for other
  basketball, or face suspension (lines 6216-6253). Players with zero to two
  years of service are exempt (line 6258); FIBA World Cup and continental
  championship participation is a conditional exception (lines 6263-6267).
- Framework implication (structural paragraph, AMENDMENT_02 Sec. 3d): the
  project's August 2, 2026 deadline sits just before the late-August World Cup
  Hiatus. The break is a hold incentive: a team may prefer roster stability into
  the break over integrating a new piece days before players scatter to national
  teams.

## 6. Maximum base salary and the supermax (Article V)

Correction to an earlier draft of this note, which wrongly claimed the WNBA has no
supermax. It does. There are two maximum base-salary tiers, both defined as a
percentage of the Salary Cap (CBA Art. V, lines 2044-2051):

- Standard Maximum Salary: 17% of the cap. For 2026 ($7M cap), $1,190,000.
- Supermax Salary: 20% of the cap. For 2026 ($7M cap), $1,400,000.

Supermax eligibility (Her Hoop Stats WNBA CBA FAQ, herhoopstats.com). A player can
reach the supermax by any of four routes:

- Rookie-scale extension: a player on a rookie-scale contract can extend effective
  for her fifth year (up to three years, through year seven), immediately at the
  supermax.
- Free-agent re-signing: a free agent with five or more years of service who
  re-signs with her prior team.
- Core designation: a free agent designated as a Core Player, regardless of years
  of service.
- Qualifying veteran extension: five or more years of service, under contract for
  part of each of the two prior seasons, subject to team-change or FA-signing
  conditions, and capped at a 20% year-one increase over the final year of the
  original contract.

Figure caveat (source conflict, resolved in favor of the CBA). The Her Hoop Stats
FAQ table (2026 supermax $256,721, standard max $220,900, at 16.5% / 14.2% of cap)
reflects the pre-2026 CBA extrapolated by 3% per year, and is superseded. The 2026
CBA raised the cap to $7M and set the tiers at 20% / 17% of the cap (CBA Art. V), so
the 2026 figures are $1.40M / $1.19M. Use the CBA figures; treat HHS as the plain-
language explainer of eligibility mechanics, not the source of current dollar
amounts. This is exactly the pre-publish checklist item 1 case: confirm cap/CBA
dollars from the primary source before publishing.

The "immobility" framing (corrects an earlier draft). AMENDMENT_02's "supermax
immobility, do not propose as a target" guidance is sound as an asset-class
judgment: a supermax player earns 20% of the cap ($1.4M of $7M, one fifth of the
roster budget) and is a franchise cornerstone, so she is not a realistic deadline
acquisition target. Keep that guidance. The basis is cost and asset class, not a
literal CBA trade ban: supermax and Core players can be traded (sign-and-trades
occur), and a Core-designated player may hold trade-approval rights (per the Her
Hoop Stats FAQ). The framework still publishes tiers, not dollars, so these max
figures mainly matter as context, not as published numbers.
